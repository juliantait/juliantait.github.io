1Information → confidence

Confidence follows the proportion of the evidence, not its weight: 3 of 3 heads 85 per cent, 6 of 9 heads 67 per cent, same posterior. Strength counts about twice weight.

With sample size randomised and beliefs paid, more balls raise uncertainty about one's own answer at a fixed posterior, and the response to evidence falls with n. The stated belief itself barely moves with mixed versus pure samples.

People compare samples by proportion and neglect sample size in 39 per cent of choices, and neglect goes with more confidence. Confidence is measured as a demand to learn, not to send.

Confidence shifted exogenously by an easy or hard test, but about one's rank, not about the data held.

Precision is assigned and drawn on the screen, so confidence is handed over, not formed.

Precision must be inferred from the chart: 20 dots leave people no more sure than 3; visible noise moves confidence, about one point per SD in both roles.

2Confidence → sharing

Confidence drives how much weight people put on their own answer (r = 0.89), and the weight is free: right answers get 75 votes, wrong ones 58.

A confidence shock raises free forwarding by 33 points; one point of belief buys 1.2 points of forwarding. The hard test silences the competent sender more than the incompetent one.

The sharing rule is a cutoff on one's own posterior, about 0.65, ignoring the partner. Common precision up: more talking, 48 to 53 per cent. Asymmetric: the precise type sits on the Bayesian cutoff, the imprecise type talks three times too often and only half learn.

A BDM price to send, against a Bayesian value per message: the informed pay for 60 per cent of what they hold, the uninformed overpay.

3Sharing → learning

Letting the confident weigh in helps the aggregate only in tasks where confidence tracks accuracy.

Receiver accuracy follows who talks: the efficient outcome, the right sender talking, happens 28 per cent of the time without feedback and 83 per cent with.

Forced draws show talk choices are on the right side on average: forcing the opposite raises error. Symmetric arms only.

The receiver's loss from unsent messages is a closed form from the senders' prices.

Claim Information does not travel in proportion to its worth.

We give one person a lot of evidence and another a little, pay both for an honest belief, and let each buy the right to pass their advice to someone who has nothing. A Bayesian pays in proportion to how far the message would move the receiver, so the well-informed should pay about twice as much. Our pilots say they will not: seven times the data leaves people no more sure, and sureness is mostly a trait of the person. If people pay by how sure they feel, the best-informed under-share and the least-informed over-share relative to what they know, and the receiver learns less than the information in the room allows. Within a role it is the noise in the chart, not the amount of evidence, that moves confidence: about one point of confidence per SD of visible noise, the same in both roles, while the side of the bet barely moves.

Contribution The first end-to-end test with the information held fixed.

Each link is known on its own. More evidence raises confidence far less than Bayes says (; ; ; ; ). Confidence drives who speaks (; ; ) and people pay for communication beyond its value (; ). Communication helps only when confidence tracks accuracy (; ; ). What nobody has done is run the chain end to end with the information held fixed by the experimenter: assign the amount of evidence, measure the belief it produces, price the decision to pass it on to someone uninformed, and score what the receiver learns against the Bayesian value of every message. That lets us say, round by round, who shares too little and who too much, whether the price follows confidence or value, and what that costs the receiver.

Two benchmarks and the pilot numbers

With aligned pay, the SENDER's value of sending is the expected improvement in the RECEIVER's score. Moving a RECEIVER from 50/50 to p is worth X(p − ½)² under a quadratic rule, or X(max(p, 1−p) − ½) if the RECEIVER places a binary bet.

Pricing by how sure you feel V_conf = X(q − ½)² q is the SENDER's own stated bet.
Pricing the information you hold V_true = X(p − ½)² p is the Bayesian posterior of the chart.

With a BDM the message arrives if and only if the stated price clears the random draw, so the RECEIVER's expected gain is V × P(c ≤ w), and under a uniform draw every point of under-pricing costs the RECEIVER V × (V − w) / c_max. The social cost of under-sharing is a closed form from the SENDERs' prices alone.

What we learnt from other people's data

Three public datasets re-analysed, all on link 1.

Order of the stream moves confidence at identical composition: +3.5 points when the majority leads throughout. Strength beats weight 2 to 1.

The look of the evidence matters.

The stated belief does not move with mixed versus pure samples at the same posterior; only cognitive uncertainty does. Response to evidence falls with n.

Noise in numeric counts does not reach the belief.

No conflict penalty once weak-signal overweighting is controlled. Three cards count for less than one.

Amount of data is discounted, conflict is not.

Reading across them: noise moves confidence in perceptual streams and charts, not in numeric counts. That is the case for scatterplots over urns.

The asymmetric arm

Reported in the April 2026 manuscript as a "robustness treatment" (pp. 11-12 and 17, Appendix C pp. 25-27).

What it is

80 subjects, fixed types A and B with precision 0.9 against 0.6, always matched with the other type, neutral labels. 30 periods, strategy-method thresholds from period 6, forced TALK and forced LISTEN in a sixth of rounds each, beliefs paid after communication.

Theory: the precise type should TALK from a hint of 0.69 outward, the imprecise type only beyond 0.93.

What the manuscript reports

  • The imprecise type talks 37.5% of the time, the precise type 55.9%, significant at the 1% level.
  • Empirically optimal cutoffs put the imprecise type at listen-to-almost-anything and the precise type at 70.
  • Learning types by signal type.

So the better-informed type does talk more, in the right direction.

What it still does not report, and what we would ask for

  • Link 3: the forced-draw value of talking in asymmetric pairs (their Table 4 is not run for this arm), the cost of each imprecise-type talk.

What it cannot tell us

Anything about a price, quantity against quality of evidence, or noise.

Read against their own benchmark our computation, not theirs

Reading: Appendix C shows where the gap sits. The precise type holds a threshold of about 71 throughout, next to the Bayesian 69 and the empirical optimum 70; the imprecise type starts near 74 and climbs to about 83 by the last period, against a Bayesian 93 and an empirical optimum of listening to everything. The informed type is calibrated; the compression is the less informed type talking far too much, as a fixed posterior cutoff that ignores the partner would produce (their footnote 10).

What this means for us: the less informed over-share even when precision is printed on the screen, so our NOVICES overpaying would replicate them. The better informed under-sharing is not in their data, because their precise type can see its precision; our EXPERT has to read it from 20 dots, and the pilots say that reading fails. Their arm is the displayed-precision control; ours is the perceived-precision treatment.

Consequence for us: the paper is about the informed sender who cannot see what they hold, priced, and charged to the receiver.
