Design

Fixed x: x = linspace(0, 100, n) — n evenly spaced observation years across a 100-year window, so every member shares the same design and the standard error SE = σ/√SSx is identical at a given n.

Randomly drawn x: xi ~ iid Uniform(0, 100) per member, so SSx (and therefore SE) varies card to card. Headline error rates and the sampling PDFs of β̂ are then expected quantities, Monte-Carlo-averaged over x designs.

y values — Integers (default, as in the experiment): each dot is the continuous draw rounded to the nearest integer at the simulation step, and every displayed number — β̂, SE, likelihoods, posteriors, picks, error statistics — is computed from those realised integers. Evidence weighting the key control

A group pools its members' slope estimates into one estimate β̄ = Σwiβ̂i / Σwi, then takes the nearest candidate. The averaged panel reports the expected error rate of that decision.

Evidence precision. Each member's evidence has precision τi = 1/SEi² (larger n ⇒ sharper). For Gaussian estimates the optimal pool is the precision-weighted mean, so "optimal" and "weight by evidence precision" coincide here.

Weights wi ∝ τiρ. ρ=1 weights fully by evidence precision (optimal); ρ=0 is equal weighting (ignores precision); ρ<0 goes counter to the evidence, over-trusting noisy strong-looking members and under-weighting the EXPERT.

The betting game
